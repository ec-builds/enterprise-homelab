# Lessons Learned

| Date | Summary | Context | Takeaway |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-07-24 | **Plan IP Addressing for Growth and Documentation** | While defining the network addressing plan, address ranges needed to be organized for infrastructure, endpoints, reserved devices, and future expansion. The documentation also needed to remain useful publicly without exposing unnecessary details about the internal network. Structured address ranges were defined instead of assigning addresses ad hoc, with capacity reserved for future infrastructure, endpoints, and network services. | An IP addressing plan should account for future growth before devices and services are deployed. Structured addressing reduces the likelihood of conflicts and future renumbering while making the environment easier to manage and document. Public documentation should communicate the design and reasoning while sanitizing internal addressing details where appropriate. |
| 2026-07-24 | **Reserve DHCP Capacity for Static Infrastructure** | While configuring DHCP, dynamically assigned clients needed to be prevented from consuming addresses intended for infrastructure and other devices requiring predictable addressing. The DHCP pool was configured as `10.0.0.150–10.0.0.249`, leaving addresses outside the dynamic pool available for static assignments and reservations. | Define DHCP scopes with intentional address space outside the dynamic pool. This preserves capacity for statically addressed infrastructure and reserved devices, reduces the risk of conflicts between dynamic and manually assigned addresses, and provides room for future growth without redesigning the DHCP scope. |
| 2026-06-13 | **Verify Guest and IoT Network Isolation** | A guest network was configured for IoT and other less-trusted devices. Intranet access was disabled to prevent those devices from reaching the primary LAN, and client isolation was tested to confirm devices on the guest network could not communicate directly with one another. This established a basic segmentation boundary pending future VLAN-based segmentation. | Network isolation should be validated rather than assumed from configuration settings. Segmentation provides meaningful protection only when the intended traffic restrictions are confirmed through testing. Guest and IoT networks should prevent unnecessary access to trusted internal resources and, where appropriate, restrict communication between untrusted clients. |
